# bh-fall: fact notes

A 40 s vertical explainer, "What if you fell into a black hole?", voiced from `script.vo.md`.

## Facts and simplifications

- **Event horizon:** the boundary inside which escape would need more than the speed of light, so
  nothing, not even light, gets out.
- **Spaghettification:** the tidal force (the difference in pull between your feet and your head)
  near a *stellar-mass* black hole is strong enough outside the horizon to stretch a body long and
  thin.
- **Supermassive black holes** like Sagittarius A* have a much gentler tidal force at the horizon, so
  an infalling person would cross it without feeling anything special.
  - Sgr A* is about 4 million solar masses, about 27,000 light-years away, at the centre of the
    Milky Way.
- **Distant observer:** from far away, time dilation and gravitational redshift make the faller
  appear to slow down, redden and fade at the horizon, never quite crossing. The faller crosses in
  finite time by their own clock.
- **Inside** a non-rotating black hole, every future-pointing path ends at the central singularity.
- **"Nothing comes back out"** is the classical picture. Hawking radiation (quantum, extremely slow
  for real black holes) is left out for length.
- **Simplified visuals:**
  - The accretion disk and its lensing are stylised.
  - The friend's clock is illustrative.
  - The tunnel inside is a metaphor, not a literal view.

**Sources:** NASA Science, "Black Holes" (science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes); Event Horizon
Telescope collaboration, first image of Sagittarius A* (12 May 2022); standard general relativity
(tidal forces, gravitational time dilation, redshift).
